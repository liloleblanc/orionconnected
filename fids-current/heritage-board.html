A winter morning at Moncton, 1991. The day's first movement is the DC-9 that came in from Toronto around midnight, carried on to Charlottetown, slept there and is working back — the last aeroplane of one day is the first of the next.

Every flight here is invented. The network is not: Air Nova to Montreal, Halifax, Fredericton and Saint John; Air Atlantic to Halifax and nowhere else; Air Canada to Toronto; Northwest Airlink to Boston on a Metro. Moncton–Saint John–Montreal really was one aeroplane serving two cities, and the winter charters to Florida really did sometimes need a fuel stop and sometimes did not.

None of that is recoverable from any surviving source. These carriers are gone and nobody publishes their schedules. It is written down here because somebody remembered standing in that terminal.
